Options 101

An option is a contract that gives you the right, but never the obligation, to buy or sell at a set price. You're buying a choice. In the next few minutes you'll meet calls and puts, break down every contract, and get a feel for the Greeks.

8 sections 3 hands-on 5-question quiz ~3.5 min
The Right, Not the Obligation

Two contracts, two directions

A call is the right to buy. A put is the right to sell.

Call

Right to buy

You lock in the right to buy the underlying at the strike. Buy calls when you're bullish and expect the price to rise.

Put

Right to sell

You lock in the right to sell the underlying at the strike. Buy puts when you're bearish and expect the price to fall.

Both are derivative contracts: their value comes from something else, the stock or asset sitting underneath them.
Strike, Expiration, Premium

The three parts of every contract

Once you can name these three, you can read any option quote. Then we add a light touch of the Greeks.

Strike, expiration, premium.

Strike is the agreed price you can buy or sell at. Expiration is the deadline, the date the contract stops existing. Premium is what you pay to own the right.

Premium splits in two: intrinsic value (real money right now) plus time value (what you pay for the chance the price moves your way before expiration).

Leverage

Control more with less

A smaller amount of capital can control a larger position. Leverage amplifies both gains and losses.

Defined risk

Buyer's max loss

When you buy an option, the most you can lose is the premium you paid, nothing beyond it.

In or Out of the Money

Is it worth something yet?

Moneyness answers one question: does the option hold real value right now, or is it all time and hope?

Call ITM

Price above the strike

A call is in-the-money when the price sits above the strike, because you'd rather buy low while the market trades higher.

Put ITM

Price below the strike

A put is in-the-money when the price sits below the strike, because you'd rather sell high than at the lower market.

Out-of-the-money is simply the opposite in each case. In-the-money options carry intrinsic value, real worth baked in today. Out-of-the-money options are all time value.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. What are options?

Options are derivative contracts whose value comes from an underlying asset.

2. Why would you buy a put option?

A put gains value when the underlying falls, so buyers use puts to profit from or hedge a decline.

3. When is a call option in-the-money (ITM)?

A call is ITM when the underlying's current price is above the strike price.

4. What is the key leverage advantage of options?

Options provide leverage: a smaller premium controls a larger position, amplifying gains and losses.

5. When you BUY an option, what is the most you can lose?

When you buy an option, the premium is your maximum loss if it expires worthless.
