Protect the Money First

The first job of an investor isn't to chase gains, it's to not get wiped out. Get sizing, stops, and reward-to-risk right and you stay in the game long enough for everything else to work.

8 sections 3 hands-on 5-question quiz ~3.5 min
Rule One

You can't compound from zero

Risk management is one thing above all: protecting your capital.

Lose it all on a single bad bet and there is nothing left to grow, no matter how good your next idea is. Survival comes before returns.
More reward

More risk

Higher potential returns almost always come with higher risk. The two move together, that is the risk-return tradeoff.

Red flag

“High return, no risk”

When someone promises big returns with no risk, treat it as the loudest warning sign there is. It usually isn't real.

Protecting the downside is what keeps you alive to catch the upside. Everything in this lesson is built on that one idea.
The 1-2% Rule

Cap what any one trade can cost you

Risk no more than one to two percent of your account on any single trade. Even a brutal losing streak can't ruin you when each loss is that small.

That thin red slice is all one trade is allowed to cost.

Ten losses in a row at one percent each still leaves most of your account intact. Small, capped losses are survivable. Big ones aren't.
Three ways to size a position
Fixed dollar

Same amount each time

Put the same set dollar amount into every position. Simple, but it ignores how far your stop sits from entry.

Percent of portfolio

Same slice each time

Put a fixed percent of the whole portfolio into each position, so size scales with the account.

Risk-based

Work backward from the loss you'll accept

Decide the dollars you're willing to lose first, then let the distance to your stop set the share count. This is the professional's method, and it's what the calculator below uses.

Stops & Reward-to-Risk

Two tools that turn the rule into action

A stop-loss caps the loss. Reward-to-risk tells you whether the setup is even worth taking.

A stop-loss sells at a preset price to cap your loss.

It's the safety net that catches a falling trade before it wrecks the account. You decide the exit before emotions get a vote.

Reward-to-risk compares the upside to your target against the downside to your stop.

Favor setups where reward clearly outweighs risk, something like two to one, so your winners more than pay for your losers.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. What is diversification in risk management?

Diversification spreads capital across different asset classes so one loss has less impact on the whole portfolio.

2. What is the risk-return tradeoff?

Higher potential returns generally require accepting higher risk; the two move together.

3. What is position sizing?

Position sizing decides how much capital goes into each position so no single one dominates the portfolio.

4. What does a stop-loss order do?

A stop-loss sells at a preset price to cap the loss if a trade moves against you.

5. The 1-2% rule says you should:

Risking only 1-2% per trade means even a string of losses can't ruin the account.
