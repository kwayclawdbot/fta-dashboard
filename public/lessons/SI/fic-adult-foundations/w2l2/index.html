The Floor & The Ceiling

Support & resistance

Support is a price floor where buying tends to stop a decline. Resistance is the ceiling where selling stalls a rise. The market remembers these levels, and returns to them.

Support is the floor buyers defend. Resistance is the ceiling sellers guard.

These levels form because the market remembers them. Where buyers stepped in before, they tend to step in again. Where sellers took over before, they tend to take over again. They are zones, not exact prices, and either can eventually break.

Conviction

Volume confirms the move

Volume is the number of shares traded. It doesn't predict the move. It tells you how much conviction is behind it. Drag the volume and watch the breakout go from suspect to believable.

Rising volume

Believe it

A move that pushes through a level on heavy volume has real buyers or sellers behind it.

Weak volume

Stay skeptical

A breakout on thin volume is suspect. It can fail and snap right back through the level.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Nail them to earn your points and prove you can read a chart.

1. What is the significance of a support level?

Support acts like a floor where buying pressure tends to halt a decline. Resistance is the opposite: the ceiling where selling stalls a rise.

2. True or False: in an uptrend, the trendline is drawn by connecting consecutive higher highs.

In an uptrend you draw the line along the higher LOWS. In a downtrend you connect the lower highs.

3. Why does volume matter when reading a price move?

A move backed by rising volume is more reliable. Volume confirms conviction: it doesn't predict the move, it validates it.

4. Which describes an uptrend?

An uptrend is a staircase up: each peak and each pullback low is higher than the one before.

5. What is a breakout, and what makes it convincing?

A breakout is a decisive close through support, resistance, or a trendline. Heavy volume behind it makes it far more believable than a thin-volume break.
