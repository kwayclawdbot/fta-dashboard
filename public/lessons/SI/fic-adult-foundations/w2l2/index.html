Floors, Ceilings & Trends

Price moves between floors and ceilings, it drifts in trends, and volume tells you which moves to believe. Learn to read those three things and a chart stops looking like noise.

The Floor & The Ceiling

Support & resistance

Support is a price floor where buying tends to stop a decline. Resistance is the ceiling where selling stalls a rise.

These levels form because the market remembers them. Where buyers stepped in before, they tend to step in again. Where sellers took over before, they tend to take over again.
Illustrative: the price path above is the shape of the idea, not a real quote. Support and resistance are zones, not exact numbers, and either level can eventually break.
Direction

The three trends

Zoom out and every chart is doing one of three things.

Uptrend

Staircase up

Higher highs and higher lows. Draw the trendline by connecting the higher lows.

Downtrend

Staircase down

Lower highs and lower lows. Draw the trendline by connecting the lower highs.

The third case is a sideways range: no clear direction, price chopping between a rough floor and ceiling. And no trend lasts forever, so trends bend, pause, and reverse.
Conviction

Volume confirms

Volume is the number of shares traded in a period. It tells you how much conviction is behind a move.

Rising volume

Believe it

A move that pushes through a level on rising volume has real buyers or sellers behind it.

Weak volume

Stay skeptical

A breakout on thin volume is suspect. It can fail and snap right back through the level.

A breakout is a decisive close through support, resistance, or a trendline. Volume does not predict the move, it confirms whether the move is real.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. What is the significance of a support level?

Support acts like a floor where buying pressure tends to halt a decline; resistance is the ceiling.

2. True or False: In an uptrend, a trendline is drawn by connecting consecutive higher highs.

False. In an uptrend, a trendline connects consecutive higher LOWS; lower highs are connected in a downtrend.

3. Why does volume matter when reading a price move?

A move backed by rising volume is more reliable; volume confirms the conviction behind price action.

4. What defines an uptrend?

An uptrend is a staircase up: each peak and each pullback low is higher than the last.

5. What is a breakout?

A breakout is a decisive close through support/resistance or a trendline; volume helps confirm it is real.
