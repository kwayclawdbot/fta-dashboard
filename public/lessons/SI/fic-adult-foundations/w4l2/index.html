A Thesis, Not a Lottery Ticket

Options can reward you or wreck you, and the difference usually comes down to one thing: did you start with a thesis, or a wish? A lottery ticket has no plan behind it. A good options trade does.

8 sections 3 hands-on 5-question quiz ~3.5 min
Start With a View

Every trade begins with a thesis

A real view has three parts: direction, magnitude, and timing.

A thesis

You can say it out loud

Direction: up or down? Magnitude: how far? Timing: by when? Finish that sentence before you risk a dollar.

A hunch

Just a feeling

No direction, no target, no deadline. That is not a thesis, it is gambling with extra steps.

Direction, magnitude, timing. If you cannot fill in all three, you do not have a trade yet, you have a wish.
Match the Play to the View

The view picks the play

Once you have a clear view, the right structure almost chooses itself. Two directional bets, plus two calmer plays for shares you already own.

Bullish or bearish? That fork sets the play.

Bullish

Buy a call

You expect the price to rise, so you buy the right to buy at the strike.

Bearish

Buy a put

You expect the price to drop, so you buy the right to sell at the strike.

Already own the shares? Two safer uses.

Protective put

Insurance on your shares

A put on stock you own works like an umbrella, cushioning the fall if the price drops.

Covered call

Income in a flat market

Sell a call against shares you own to collect income while the market drifts sideways or edges up.

Match the play to the view, not to the excitement. The structure should serve your thesis, never your adrenaline.
These plays are teaching examples, not trade advice. Any figure you meet later in the course is illustrative, the shape of the idea, not a forecast or a promise.
Why Buyers Blow Up

Time is working against you

Buying options is defined-risk, but it comes with a clock. Understand the clock and you sidestep the most common way beginners get wiped out.

Time decay, theta, chips away at your premium every day.

If the move you predicted does not show up before expiration, the option can expire worthless, and you lose the entire premium you paid.

Those cheap, far out-of-the-money contracts that promise the moon are the trap. Most of them expire worthless.

Defined risk

Small and sized

Keep each position small and the risk defined. Never put on more than you can calmly lose.

Give it room

Enough time

Buy enough time to expiration so your thesis has a fair chance to play out before the clock runs down.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. What does a covered call let you do?

A covered call sells a call against shares you own to generate income, best in a flat or mildly bullish market.

2. What is the primary purpose of a protective put?

A protective put acts like insurance on shares you own, cushioning losses if the price drops.

3. You expect a stock to climb over the next month. Which fits your view?

A bullish outlook fits buying a call, which profits if the price rises.

4. What is the key risk of BUYING an option?

Options have a deadline; if the underlying doesn't move past the strike in time, the option can expire worthless.

5. Before risking real money on an options strategy, a smart beginner should:

Paper trading rehearses the strategy and builds discipline without risking real capital.
