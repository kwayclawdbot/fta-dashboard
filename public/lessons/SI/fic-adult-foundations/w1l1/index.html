Put Your Money to Work

Idle money quietly loses ground as the cost of living rises. Investing is how you put it to work. In the next few minutes you'll see exactly why time and compounding change everything.

The Foundation

Give your money a job

Saving keeps money safe and flat. Investing gives it a job — a slice of something that can grow. Left alone as cash, the same money quietly buys a little less every year.

Saving

Safe & flat

Perfect for an emergency fund and money you may need soon. But safe money mostly stays flat, and flat loses to rising prices over time.

Investing

Owned & growing

You own a piece of real companies that can become worth more. That chance to grow — and to compound — is the entire point.

The Compounding Engine

Watch one line pull away

Two lines begin with the same dollars. Watch the invested line pull away, year after year. That widening gap is compounding — returns earning their own returns.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Nail them to earn your points and prove you've got the why.

1. What happens to money that just sits idle in a bank account over time?

Idle cash buys a little less each year as the cost of living creeps up. That quiet erosion is the reason we invest.

2. What is the best one-line definition of investing?

Investing means giving your money a job — putting it into assets like shares so it can grow over time. Saving just keeps money flat and safe.

3. What does "compounding" actually mean?

Compounding is returns earning returns — your gains start making gains. Barely visible early on; given decades, the invested line pulls far ahead.

4. The 7% growth rate shown in the compounding examples is best described as:

The rate is illustrative — it shows the shape of the idea. Real markets rise and fall, and no one can guarantee a return.

5. Which is a reasonable investing goal rather than a gamble?

Reasonable goals play out over years and decades — exactly the timeframe compounding rewards. Get-rich-this-week targets are gambles, not goals.
