Why Invest

Idle money quietly loses ground as the cost of living rises. Investing is how you put it to work. In the next few minutes you'll see exactly why time and compounding change everything.

The Foundation

Give your money a job

Investing means giving your money a job — putting it into assets like shares of a company and letting it grow over time.

Saving

Safe & flat

Keeps your money available for emergencies. That matters. But safe money mostly stays flat.

Investing

Owned & growing

You own a piece of something that can become worth more. That chance to grow is the whole point.

Flat versus growing. That single difference is the reason this course exists.

Left alone, cash doesn't just sit there — it slowly buys less.

As prices creep up year after year, idle money loses buying power. Investing is how you give it a fighting chance to keep up and pull ahead.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. What happens to money that just sits idle in a bank account over time?

Idle cash buys a little less each year as the cost of living creeps up. That quiet erosion is why we invest.

2. What is the best one-line definition of investing?

Investing means giving your money a job — putting it into assets like shares so it can grow over time. That's different from saving, which just keeps money flat and safe.

3. What does "compounding" actually mean?

Compounding is returns earning returns — your gains start making gains. Early on it's barely visible; given decades, the invested line pulls far ahead.

4. The 7% growth rate shown in the compounding chart is best described as:

The rate is illustrative — it shows the shape of the idea. Real markets rise and fall, and no one can guarantee a return.

5. Which is a reasonable investing goal rather than a gamble?

Reasonable goals play out over years and decades — exactly the timeframe compounding rewards. Get-rich-this-week targets are gambles, not goals.
