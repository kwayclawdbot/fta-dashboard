Reading Candles

Anatomy of a Candle

Four prices in one shape

A candlestick shows one period's price action. The body spans the open and the close. The wicks, or shadows, reach up to the high and down to the low.

Illustrative candle, not a real quote. It shows the shape of the parts, not specific prices.

One candle packs four prices into a single shape: open, high, low, and close.

A plain line chart gives you only the close. A candle shows the full range and the fight inside it, one round of a tug-of-war between buyers and sellers.

Why candles over a plain line? More information per period.

A line only traces the closing price. A bar or a candle records where price opened, how high and low it swung, and where it ended, all in one mark you can read at a glance.
Green vs Red

Color and size tell the story

The body's color says who finished in control. Its length says how hard they pushed.

Green / white body

Close above open

Buyers won the period. Price finished higher than it started, so demand carried it.

Red / black body

Close below open

Sellers took over. Price finished lower than it started, so supply pressed it down.

Body length measures pressure.

A long body signals strong, one-sided pressure, an intense battle with a clear winner. A short body means the open and close landed close together, low volatility and a balanced fight with no decisive result.
Same Stock, Many Lenses

One stock, three distances

The same stock looks different on a weekly, daily, or hourly chart. Each is the same picture seen from a different distance.

Zoom out for the trend. Zoom in for the detail.

The weekly view shows the whole range, the daily the middle ground, the hourly the fine detail. Same mountain, different distance.

Read more than one on purpose.

A signal on the hourly chart carries more weight when the daily and weekly agree with it. That's multiple-timeframe analysis: checking a short-term move against the broader context of a bigger chart.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. On a candlestick, what does a green (or white) body indicate?

A green/white body means the close was above the open, so buyers dominated that period.

2. What do the wicks (shadows) of a candlestick show?

Wicks extend from the body to the period's high and low, showing the extremes and volatility.

3. True or False: A long body on a candlestick indicates less volatility.

False. A long body signals strong buying or selling pressure; a short body suggests low volatility.

4. What does a single candlestick mainly reveal about its period?

Each candle shows who won the period, buyers (green) or sellers (red), and how hard they fought via the wicks.

5. Why review the same stock on more than one timeframe?

Multiple-timeframe analysis confirms a short-term signal against the bigger-picture trend.
