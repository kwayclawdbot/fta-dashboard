What a Stock Is

Buy a stock and you own a real slice of a real business. In the next few minutes you'll see exactly what a share is, and how the market that trades it actually works.

8 sections 3 hands-on 5-question quiz ~3.5 min
The Definition

You own a slice

A stock is a security that represents proportionate ownership in a company.

Buy a single share and you become a shareholder, with a real claim on that company's assets and its earnings. Own more shares, own more of the pie.

Dividends

A share of the profits

Some companies return part of their profits to shareholders as dividends, cash paid out on a regular schedule.

No minimum

One share is enough

There's no hundred-share minimum. A single share already makes you a part-owner of the business.

Ownership, not a loan. That distinction is the foundation of everything else in this course.
The Marketplace

How the market works

The stock market isn't one building. It's a network of exchanges and brokers connecting buyers and sellers.

Going public

The IPO

A private company first sells shares to the public through an Initial Public Offering, or IPO, to raise money.

After the IPO

Trading on the exchange

Once shares are public, they trade freely between investors on an exchange, day after day.

Buyers and sellers rarely meet. Exchanges and brokers route the orders that connect them.

The Scoreboard

Reading the indexes

An index is a snapshot of one part of the market.

No one tracks every stock at once. Instead we watch indexes, each a basket that tells you how its slice of the market is doing.

Dow Jones (DJIA)30 large U.S. companies
S&P 500500 largest U.S. companies
Nasdaq CompositeTech-heavy
When the news says "the market" rose or fell, it usually means one of these indexes moved.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. What does a stock represent?

A stock is ownership in the issuing company plus a proportionate claim on its assets and earnings.

2. A company pays you every quarter for being a shareholder. What are those payments called?

Regular payments to shareholders are dividends. Capital gains come from selling at a higher price; bonds are loans.

3. How does a private company first become available to trade on an exchange?

An IPO is the process by which a company first issues stock to the public to raise funds.

4. Which index tracks 30 large U.S. companies across various sectors?

The DJIA tracks 30 large U.S. companies. The S&P 500 tracks 500; the Nasdaq Composite is tech-heavy.

5. What primarily moves a stock's price up or down day to day?

When more people want to buy than sell, price rises; when sellers outnumber buyers, it falls.
