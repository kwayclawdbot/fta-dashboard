The Definition

A stock is a slice of ownership

A stock is a security that represents proportionate ownership in a company. Buy a share and you become a part-owner, with a real claim on its assets and earnings. Own more shares, own more of the pie.

25%of the company
Illustrative company · 1,000 shares · $50 each
You own $12,500 — a 25% stake

Own more shares, own more of the pie. Real companies split into millions of shares, so a single share is a tiny — but real — slice of the whole business.

Dividends

A share of the profits

Some companies return part of their profits to shareholders as dividends, cash paid out on a regular schedule.

No minimum

One share is enough

There's no hundred-share minimum. A single share already makes you a part-owner — ownership, not a loan.

The Scoreboard

Reading the market's indexes

No one tracks every stock at once. Instead we watch indexes, each a basket of companies that tells you how its slice of the market is doing. They vary hugely in how many companies they hold.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points and prove you've got the what.

1. What does a stock represent?

A stock is ownership in the issuing company plus a proportionate claim on its assets and earnings. That's the opposite of a loan.

2. A company pays you every quarter for being a shareholder. What are those payments called?

Regular payments to shareholders are dividends. Capital gains come from selling at a higher price; bonds and interest belong to lending, not owning.

3. How does a private company first become available to trade on an exchange?

An IPO is the process by which a company first issues stock to the public to raise funds. After that, shares trade freely on the exchange.

4. Which index tracks 30 large U.S. companies across various sectors?

The DJIA tracks 30 large U.S. companies. The S&P 500 tracks 500; the Nasdaq Composite holds around 3,000 and skews tech-heavy.

5. What primarily moves a stock's price up or down day to day?

When more people want to buy than sell, the price rises; when sellers outnumber buyers, it falls. That's supply and demand at work.
