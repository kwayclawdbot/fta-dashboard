Read the Chart's Clues

Charts leave clues. Patterns and indicators are the tools that read them, hinting at what price might do next. Never guaranteeing it. In the next few minutes you'll learn the headline few and how to read them honestly.

Patterns That Repeat

Shapes the crowd keeps drawing

Because human behavior repeats, a handful of shapes show up again and again. Pick one below and watch it draw — each carries its own bias.

Pattern reader · illustrative shapes
Head & Shoulders Bearish

Three peaks, a taller head between two lower shoulders. A break below the neckline warns the uptrend may be turning over.

Reversal

Trend may turn

Head and shoulders, double top, double bottom. Price tests a level and either rejects it or holds — hinting the trend could flip.

Continuation

Trend may resume

Wedges and flags. A brief pause inside a move that often gives way to the trend picking back up.

The confirming tell: a breakout means more when volume rises with it. A move on weak volume is far easier to fade.
Moving Averages

Turn a jagged line into a signal

A moving average plots the average price over a chosen window, so the noise becomes a smooth line. Watch a short average cross a long one — the classic momentum tell.

50-day vs 200-day · illustrative
Bullish Short MA is +4.2% above the long MA
Price Long MA (slow) Short MA (fast)
SMA

Steady & even

The simple moving average treats every period the same. Smoother, and slower to turn.

EMA

Quick & recent

The exponential moving average weights recent prices more, so it responds sooner to a shift.

Same two lines, opposite meaning. What matters is which way the shorter average crosses the longer one — up reads bullish, down reads bearish.
Momentum Meters

Reading momentum, not the future

Oscillators measure how fast and how far price has moved, swinging inside a fixed range. The RSI runs 0 to 100. Above 70 is generally overbought; below 30, generally oversold.

RSI

70 / 30

Above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold. A momentum reading from 0 to 100.

Stochastic

80 / 20

Above 80 overbought, below 20 oversold. Same idea, tighter bands.

◆ Coach note

Before you try the meters yourself, watch how momentum and a moving-average cross line up in real time. I'll pause the narration the moment you press play.

See it? No single meter is a crystal ball — but when momentum and the trend point the same way, the read is far more trustworthy. Hit Continue whenever you're ready.

Honest limit: a stock can stay overbought far longer than feels reasonable. These meters read the temperature, not the future.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Answer them to earn your points for the lesson.

1. Which pattern signals a potential trend change and consists of three peaks?

The head and shoulders has three peaks (a higher middle head between two shoulders) and often signals a reversal.

2. What is the main difference between an SMA and an EMA?

The EMA weights recent prices more heavily, so it responds faster than the equally weighted SMA.

3. When does a golden cross occur?

A golden cross (bullish) is when a shorter MA crosses above a longer MA; the reverse is a death cross.

4. On the RSI oscillator, a reading above 70 generally signals the stock is:

RSI runs 0-100; above 70 is generally overbought, below 30 generally oversold.

5. What most helps confirm that a chart-pattern breakout is real?

A breakout backed by increased volume is more trustworthy than one on weak volume.
