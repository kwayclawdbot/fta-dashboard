Your real opponent is you

The hardest part of investing is not the charts. It is the person holding the phone. Beat fear and greed with two unstoppable tools: a written plan, and an honest journal.

The Two Villains

Fear and greed

Two emotions end more accounts than any bad chart ever will.

Greed

Buy high

Everyone is hyped, so you jump in near the top, scared of missing out.

Fear

Sell low

It dips, the mood turns dark, so you dump it near the bottom.

Buy high, sell low: the exact opposite of the plan. Emotional resilience is not feeling nothing, it is refusing to let a loud feeling place the order.

Your Journal Is Your Coach

Write it down, then review it

Every trade gets logged. Over time your journal shows you the truth about your own habits.

Log five things every time: why in, the plan, the exit, the stop, and what happened.

Then read it back. The journal is a mirror. It shows the mistakes you keep repeating and the setups that actually work for you. Reviewed honestly, it is the best coach you will ever have, and it works for free.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Show me you can keep your head and trust the plan.

1. Fear and greed usually push traders to:

Greed makes people buy at the top and fear makes them sell at the bottom, which is buy high, sell low, exactly backwards.

2. What is the point of a written trade plan (entry, exit, stop)?

When entry, exit, and stop are decided in advance, fear and greed do not get a vote.

3. What belongs in a trading journal?

Logging your reasoning and the outcome lets you review, spot repeated mistakes, and improve.

4. Why is cutting a small loss quickly considered smart?

Small losses protect your money for the next opportunity; big losses are what end accounts.

5. Someone promises a guaranteed, risk-free 50% return. You should:

No real investment guarantees profits; a promise of high returns with zero risk is a red flag.
