The Two Villains

Fear and greed run the show

Two emotions wreck more traders than any bad chart ever will. Greed buys the top. Fear sells the bottom. Same $1,000, one wild year — watch what each one does with it.

$1,000 to start · one year · illustrative
Feelings shrink it. A plan grows it.
Traded on feelings  ·  Traded on a plan  ·  dashed line = your starting $1,000
Greed

Buy high

Everyone's hyped, so you jump in at the top — scared of missing out. You pay the most right before it cools off.

Fear

Sell low

It dips, everyone panics, so you dump it at the bottom. You lock in the loss right before it recovers.

Buy high, sell low: the exact opposite of a good plan. Feelings are loud, and they're usually wrong at the worst possible moment.

Decide first

Entry, exit, stop

Write down where you'll buy, where you'll take profit, and where you'll bail — before you're in the trade and emotional.

Then obey

Follow the paper

When the market gets loud, you don't guess. You already made the calm decision. You just follow it.

Your Journal Is Your Coach

Write it down, then review it

Every trade gets logged. Over time your journal becomes a mirror — it shows you the mistakes you keep repeating and the setups that actually work for you. And it's free.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Show me you can keep your cool and trust the plan.

1. Fear and greed usually push traders to:

Greed makes people buy at the top and fear makes them sell at the bottom. That's buy high, sell low — exactly backwards.

2. What is the point of a written trade plan (entry, exit, stop)?

When entry, exit, and stop are decided in advance, fear and greed don't get a vote. Plans beat feelings.

3. Why is cutting a small loss quickly considered a win?

Small losses protect your money for the next opportunity. Big losses are what end accounts.

4. What belongs in a trading journal?

Logging your reasoning and the outcome lets you review, spot repeated mistakes, and actually improve. The journal is your coach.

5. Someone online promises a guaranteed, risk-free 50% return. You should:

No real investment guarantees profits. A promise of high returns with zero risk is a red flag.
