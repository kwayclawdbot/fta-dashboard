How the stock market actually works

Roblox. Nike. Apple. You already know these companies. This lesson turns stuff you use every day into stuff you can actually own a piece of.

7 sections 3 hands-on 5-question quiz ~2.5 min
The Big Idea

You can own a slice

A stock is a share of ownership in a real company. Buy one share and you literally own a tiny piece of it.

Price gains

It can grow

If the company does well and more people want in, your slice can be worth more than you paid.

Dividends

It can pay you

Some companies send shareholders a small cash payment, often each quarter, just for holding.

You don't need 100 shares to count. One share makes you a real part-owner, a shareholder.
The Matching System

Not one building — a network

The stock market isn't a place you walk into. It's a giant matching system that pairs buyers with sellers all day long.

When you want to buy and someone wants to sell, the market matches you. That's the whole engine.

Every company gets a short ticker symbol so there's no mix-up. Apple is AAPL. Nike is NKE. That little code is how you find and trade it.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Show me you can spot a real company and how the market moves.

1. What is a stock?

A stock is a share of ownership. Buy one and you own a tiny slice of the company behind a product you might already use.

2. What is Apple's ticker symbol?

Apple trades under AAPL. A ticker is the short code that names a stock so there's no mix-up.

3. What mostly moves a stock's price up or down?

More buyers than sellers pushes price up; more sellers than buyers pushes it down. That tug-of-war is the whole engine.

4. What is the stock market, really?

It's not one building. It's a giant network of exchanges, a matching system pairing buyers and sellers all day.

5. You buy 1 share of a company you love. Which is TRUE?

One share makes you a real part-owner. There's no 100-share minimum, and no investment is guaranteed to grow.
