What a Stock Really Is

You can own a slice

A stock is a tiny piece of a real company. Buy one share and you're a part-owner — when the company does well, your slice can grow, and some companies even pay owners a little cash. Slide the shares and watch your stake.

Your stake: 1 share
A tiny slice — but a real one
It can grow

Your slice gains value

If the company gets bigger and more valuable, each share you own is worth more than you paid. That's how investors grow money over years.

It can pay you

Some pay a dividend

Certain companies share a slice of their profits with owners, in small cash payments called dividends — just for holding the stock.

Not One Building — a Network

How a trade actually happens

The "stock market" isn't one place. It's a network of exchanges — giant computers that match a buyer to a seller in a fraction of a second. Here's the path every share takes.

Lock It In

Quick check

Four questions. Nail them to prove you've got how the market works.

1. What is a stock, in plain terms?

A stock (share) is a unit of ownership. Buy one and you own a small slice of that company.

2. The "stock market" is best described as:

The market is a network of exchanges — computers that instantly match a buyer to a seller.

3. On a trading screen, what is a ticker symbol?

The ticker is the symbol — like NKE for Nike or RBLX for Roblox — used to identify the stock.

4. Your friend loves a sneaker brand and wants to own part of it. How could they?

If the brand is a public company, anyone can buy shares of it through the market and own a real slice.
