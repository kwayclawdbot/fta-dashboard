Floors, ceilings, and the trend

Charts aren't random. Price keeps stopping at the same spots, like there's an invisible floor holding it up and an invisible ceiling pushing it down. Find those, and you stop guessing.

Support & Resistance

Support and resistance

Price stops at the same spots again and again. Those spots have names.

A floor holds price up. A ceiling caps it.

Support

The floor

Buyers keep stepping in here, so the fall stalls and price bounces.

Resistance

The ceiling

Sellers keep showing up here, so the rise stalls and price gets pushed back.

These are zones, not exact lines. Treat a level like a neighborhood, not a single address. The more bounces, the more people are watching it.
Trend & Role Reversal

The trend, and a level that flips

Zoom out and price leans a direction. Learn to name it.

Break a ceiling, and it can become the new floor.

Uptrend

Higher highs & lows

Buyers stacking the staircase upward. Trade with it.

Downtrend

Lower highs & lows

Sellers pressing the staircase down.

Sideways between the floor and ceiling is a range. And when price finally breaks resistance, that old ceiling often flips into support. Trade with the trend, not against it.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Find the floor, the ceiling, and the trend.

1. Support is best described as:

Support is a floor. Buyers step in there, so the fall stalls and price tends to bounce.

2. A stock keeps getting pushed back down every time it reaches the same price. That level is:

A level that repeatedly caps the rise is resistance, the ceiling where sellers show up.

3. An uptrend is made of:

An uptrend is a staircase up: each high and each low lands above the last.

4. A stock breaks above a ceiling it was stuck under for months, then pulls back and holds right at that old level. What likely happened?

Role reversal: once price breaks resistance, that old ceiling often becomes the new floor.

5. Price is bouncing between the same floor and ceiling with no clear direction. This is a:

No clear direction, stuck between support and resistance, is a range.
