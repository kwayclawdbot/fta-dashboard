Never risk more than a sliver

The number-one survival rule in trading: keep only about 1 to 2 percent of your account on the line in any single trade. Learn to size a position, and one bad call can never take you out.

7 sections 3 hands-on 5-question quiz ~3 min
The Survival Rule

Only a sliver is ever on the line

Great traders are wrong all the time. What keeps them alive is that a wrong call only ever costs them a small, planned slice of the account.

Risk about 1 to 2 percent of your account per trade. No more.

Small loss

You stay in the game

Wrong on a 2 percent trade? You lose a sliver and calmly take the next setup.

Oversized bet

One wrong call ends it

All-in with no plan? A single bad trade can swallow the whole account.

This was never about being right every time. It's about surviving every time you're wrong, so you're still here when the great setups show up.
Sizing the Trade

How many shares? Let the risk decide.

A stop-loss is your exit, decided before you buy.

Risk per share

Entry minus stop

Plan to buy at 50 and set your stop at 48? That's 2 dollars of risk on every share.

Share count

Dollars at risk ÷ risk per share

Take your account times 1 or 2 percent, then divide by the risk per share. That's how many shares fit.

Move your stop closer to your entry and each share risks less, so the same small slice buys you more shares. That is position sizing doing its job.
The numbers here are illustrative teaching tools built from values you choose. They plan your risk. They are not a forecast or a promise about any real stock.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five scenarios. Show me the survival rule stuck.

1. What is the number-one job of the 1-2% rule?

The rule caps how much you can lose on any single trade, so being wrong once never takes you out of the game.

2. A stop-loss order is:

A stop-loss is your exit, decided in advance. If price hits that level you're out, which caps the loss.

3. You plan to enter at $50 and set your stop at $48. How much are you risking per share?

Risk per share is entry minus stop: 50 minus 48 is 2 dollars on every share.

4. Position sizing is how you decide:

Position sizing sets your share count so that if the stop hits, your loss stays inside your 1-2% slice.

5. Two traders each get a call wrong. Trader A risked 2% with a stop; Trader B went all-in with no stop. What's the likely difference?

Small, controlled losses keep you in the game. One oversized, unprotected bet can end it. Surviving the wrong trades is the whole point.
