Beat, Miss or Priced In

Price moves on the surprise

A company can post great numbers and still drop. The secret is expectations. Watch the gap between what the crowd expected and what actually happened: that gap is the surprise.

Earnings per share · one quarter · illustrative
Actual $1.45 vs expected $1.20: a beat.
What the crowd expected  ·  What actually happened
Beat

Actual > expected

A positive surprise. The stock usually pops, sometimes hard.

Miss

Actual < expected

A letdown. The stock usually dumps, even if profit still grew.

When people say a result was already priced in, they mean the good news was expected, so it was baked into the price before the report even landed.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Prove you can read what moves a stock.

1. What is a “catalyst” in the stock market?

A catalyst is news or an event, like an earnings report or a product launch, that can move a stock quickly.

2. A company reports strong earnings, but the stock DROPS. What is the most likely reason?

Price moves on the surprise versus expectations. If the crowd expected more, even good results disappoint. It was already priced in.

3. What does EPS (earnings per share) tell you?

EPS is profit divided by the number of shares. Higher EPS generally means a more profitable company.

4. A HIGH price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio usually means investors are:

A high P/E means investors pay more per dollar of earnings, usually because they expect strong growth ahead.

5. Why check trading VOLUME when a stock jumps on news?

A big move on strong volume means real conviction. The same move on weak volume is easy to distrust.
