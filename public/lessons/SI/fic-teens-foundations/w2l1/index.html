Anatomy of a Candle

Four prices, one story

Every candle packs four prices: the open, the close, the high, and the low. The color tells you who won the battle.

Length & Zoom

How hard did they win?

Color says who won. Length says how hard.

Long body

Strong & one-sided

One side had real force that period. Big win.

Long wick

Pushed & rejected

Price shoved one way, then got slapped back. A short body means it was almost a tie.

Timeframe matters too. The same stock looks choppy on a 1-minute chart and smooth on a daily. Zoom out for the trend, zoom in for detail, and make sure they agree.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Show me you can read the scoreboard.

1. On a candlestick chart, a GREEN candle usually means:

Green means the close landed above the open, so the price rose and the buyers won the battle.

2. On a candle, what do the thin lines (wicks) show?

Wicks reach up to the high and down to the low, showing how far the fight swung before it settled.

3. A long candle body tells you that period had:

A long body means one side had real force. A short body means it was a close fight, almost a tie.

4. In an uptrend, the price makes:

An uptrend is a staircase up: each peak and each dip lands higher than the last.

5. Why do traders check more than one timeframe?

The same stock looks choppy zoomed in and smooth zoomed out. Checking both keeps a signal in context.
