Myth, Cleared

A network, not a building

The stock market isn't one hall with a bell. It's a network of exchanges, like the New York Stock Exchange and the Nasdaq, all connected electronically.

Selling limited-edition sneakers? You don't stand on a corner. You list them on a marketplace app and it finds the buyer. An exchange is that app, for shares.
Inside the Machine

Buy meets sell in the middle

You tap buy; someone out there taps sell. The exchange's matching engine pairs the two orders at a price you both accept, usually in under a second.

Press the button to route an order.
The Name Tags

Tickers: the market's address system

Every listed company goes by a ticker symbol: a short code so nothing gets mixed up. Search the ticker and you're telling the matching system exactly whose shares you want.

2. When you tap "buy" on one share, who is on the other side of your trade?

The matching happens electronically, usually in under a second, millions of times a day.
