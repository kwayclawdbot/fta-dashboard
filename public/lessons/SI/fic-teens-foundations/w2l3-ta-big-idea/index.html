Two Ways to Judge a Stock

Read the company… or read the crowd

Same stock, two completely different lenses.

Fundamental analysis

The report card

Read the company: its sales, its profits, its plans. Is the business itself strong, and what is it really worth?

Technical analysis

The crowd

Read the price: ignore the press releases and study what buyers and sellers are actually doing, right now, on the chart.

The Core Belief

"Market behavior tends to repeat"

Fear looks like fear on every chart. Greed looks like greed. So the patterns price carved out before can hint at what comes next. Notice the word: hint.

What TA claims

Probabilities

"This pattern usually leans that way." Chart reading stacks the odds — it tilts your guesses from coin-flips toward educated calls.

What TA never claims

Promises

It is not a crystal ball, and anyone selling it as one is lying to you. No pattern says "this must happen." Ever.

Lock It In

Quick check

Four questions and this one's yours.

1. Technical analysis is the skill of…

TA reads what price is doing — the recorded fight between buyers and sellers — not the company's report card.

2. The core idea that makes chart reading useful is:

Crowd emotions repeat, so the patterns they carve into price tend to repeat too. That's the whole bet.

3. What does a chart pattern honestly give you?

TA deals in probabilities, never promises. It stacks the odds; it doesn't see the future.

4. "Did the company's profit grow this year?" is a question for…

Profit, sales and debt are the company's report card — the fundamental lens. The chart is the technical lens.
