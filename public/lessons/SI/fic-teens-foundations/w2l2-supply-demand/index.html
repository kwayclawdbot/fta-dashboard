The Tug-of-War Behind Every Price

Last lesson the machine matched your buy with a sell. But nobody at the exchange picked the price. So who did? One idea explains every rally, crash and boring day: supply and demand.

Demand vs. Supply

Two teams, one rope

On one end: everyone who wants to buy, that's demand. On the other: everyone who wants to sell, that's supply. The price is wherever the rope sits.

Demand · buyers

They pull the price UP

A sneaker drops in a run of 100 pairs and 10,000 people want one. The resale price rockets. Scarce thing + fighting buyers = higher price. Stocks work the same.

Supply · sellers

They drag the price DOWN

When holders rush for the exit and buyers hang back, each seller must accept a little less to get filled, and the price stair-steps down.

The Only Rule

Imbalance moves price

Not magic, not luck: an imbalance.

More buyers than sellers → price goes up.
More sellers than buyers → price goes down.

Lock It In

Quick check

Four questions on the tug-of-war.

1. What actually moves a stock's price?

Supply and demand: more buyers than sellers pushes price up; more sellers than buyers pushes it down. That's it.

2. 100 limited sneakers, 10,000 people want a pair. The resale price…

Scarcity plus heavy demand means buyers must offer more to win the pair. Stocks behave the same way.

3. Bad news hits and holders rush to sell while buyers hang back. Price…

To find a buyer, each seller has to undercut: that's how an imbalance walks a price down.

4. A price that isn't moving much tells you:

A flat price is a balanced rope: neither team is currently stronger.
