Never trade without a plan

This is the last piece before you go from learning to doing. The pros never break one rule: no trade without a written plan. Today you build your own first-trade checklist.

Trader or Gambler

A plan, not a hope

Same stock, same chart, two totally different moves.

Plan

Decided in advance

Why, entry, exit, stop, and size are all set before you click buy.

Gamble

Pure hope

You buy because it's hyped and just hope. No exit, no stop, no plan.

A plan doesn't make you right every time. It makes you disciplined every time. Discipline is what actually wins.

Your Two Safety Boxes

The stop and the size protect you

Two boxes on the checklist keep one bad trade from wrecking your account. Set both before you buy.

Illustrative example, not a real price, a forecast, or a promise. The numbers just show how a stop sits below your entry.

Your stop is a safety net you set on purpose.

Decide your stop before you buy, and fear never gets a vote later. Then keep your position small enough that a loss stings but never wrecks you. Small size is how you stay in the game long enough to get good.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Show me you can think like someone with a plan, not someone with a hunch.

1. The first-trade checklist has you decide four things before you buy. Which four?

A real plan answers why you're in, where you enter, where you take profit (exit), and where you cut the loss (stop).

2. Why does "set your stop before you buy" make the checklist?

A stop-loss is your safety net. Decide it up front so fear doesn't decide it for you later.

3. Position sizing means:

Keep every position small enough to survive a loss. Small size keeps you in the game to trade another day.

4. A stock is "mooning" and everyone says buy NOW or miss out. You have no entry, exit, or stop written down. The checklist says:

If you can't state your entry, exit, and stop, you don't have a plan. No plan, no trade.

5. After you place the order, the final box on the checklist is to:

Logging why you entered and what happened turns each trade into a lesson. Your journal is a free coach.
