A Plan, Not a Hope

Deciding in advance beats reacting

A plan move is decided before you buy, when you're calm. A gamble move is decided mid-trade, when you're excited or scared. Watch how often each one actually sticks to its exit.

The Six Boxes

Fill all six before a dollar moves

Every first trade gets the same six boxes. If you can't fill one honestly, that's your signal to skip the trade — no shame, no FOMO.

1

The name

What are you buying, and why this one? One clear reason.

2

Entry

The price you're willing to pay to get in.

3

Exit (target)

Where you'll take your win and walk away happy.

4

Stop

The price where you admit you're wrong and get out.

5

Size

How many shares, so one loss stays small.

6

The reason

One sentence: why this trade, right now.

Six boxes, every time. The checklist isn't there to slow you down — it's there so a bad day can't talk you into a bad trade.
Stop & Size

The stop and the size protect you

Two boxes do the heavy lifting. Your stop caps how far a trade can fall. Your size makes sure that fall stays small. Slide the numbers and watch your money-at-risk stay tiny.

Lock It In

Quick check

Four questions. Nail them and you've earned your first-trade checklist.

1. Why does every trade need a written plan?

A plan is decided in advance, when you're calm. In the trade, fear and excitement are loud — the written plan keeps them from making your calls.

2. Which of these belongs in your first-trade checklist?

The six boxes include the name, entry, exit (target), stop, size, and your one-sentence reason. Entry, exit, and stop are the core three.

3. What is the job of your stop price?

The stop is the price where you admit you're wrong and get out. It caps the loss so one bad trade can't wreck your account.

4. You can't honestly fill one of the six boxes. What's the smart move?

If a box won't fill honestly, that's your signal to pass. Skipping a trade is always free. No plan, no trade.
