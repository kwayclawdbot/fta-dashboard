Set It Up, Place an Order

One minute to set up

Set a starting balance, then treat that pretend money exactly like it is real.

Same position sizing, same rules, same stop. No cheating and no all-in hero bets just because it is fake, or you learn nothing worth keeping. Then you place practice orders. Every order ticket asks for the same handful of things: the symbol, buy or sell, the quantity, the order type, and often a stop.

Market order

Speed

Fills right now at whatever the current price is. Fast, but the exact price can shift a little.

Limit order

Price

Fills only at your chosen price or better. You control the price, but it only happens if the market reaches it.

Speed versus price. Then track every single result, win or lose, so you can actually get better.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Show me the setup stuck.

1. Your friend says they have been trading all week in an app, but never added any real money. What are they actually doing?

Paper trading rehearses real decisions with pretend money, so you can learn and make mistakes for free before risking real cash.

2. You open a paper account with a pretend balance. What is the smartest way to use it?

Treating pretend money like real money is the whole point. Cheating on a simulator teaches you habits that lose real cash later.

3. The market is moving fast and you want in right now, at whatever the current price is. Which order type fits?

A market order fills immediately at the current price. It is fast, though the exact fill price can shift a little.

4. You will only buy at your target price or better, and you are fine waiting. Which order gives you that control?

A limit order lets you control the price, but the trade only happens if the market actually reaches your number.

5. You are filling out a practice order ticket. Which set of details does it ask for?

Every order ticket asks for the symbol, buy or sell, the quantity, the order type, and often a stop.
