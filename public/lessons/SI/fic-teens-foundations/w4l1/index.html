Calls & puts, explained

An option is a ticket that gives you a right, not a promise. Learn the two kinds, calls and puts, and you can bet on a stock going up or down without owning a single share.

7 sections 3 hands-on 5-question quiz ~3 min
The Big Idea

An option is a contract

It gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell 100 shares of a stock at a set price before a set date. You own the right. You never have to use it.

Every contract carries the same three parts.

Strike

The locked-in price

The set price where you can buy or sell, no matter where the market goes.

Expiration

The deadline

Your window to act. After this date the contract is done.

The premium is what you pay to own the ticket in the first place. If you bought the option, that premium is the most you can lose.
Two Flavors

Call or put?

Your outlook picks the tool. Think the price goes up, or down?

Call · bet UP

The right to BUY

Grab a call when you think the price is climbing. It's like a coupon that locks in today's price before the stock rises, so you win if it goes up.

Put · bet DOWN

The right to SELL

Grab a put when you think the price is falling. It's like insurance that pays off when the stock drops, cushioning the move down.

Bullish, expecting up? That points to a call. Bearish, expecting down? That points to a put. Real companies like Nike, Apple, Roblox, or Spotify can go either way. Your read on the direction is what picks the contract.
Options are an advanced, higher-risk tool. This lesson is conceptual and paper-trade practice only. No prices or returns here are real, and none are a forecast or a promise.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions on calls, puts, and the contract. Show me it clicked.

1. A call option gives you the right to:

A call is the right to BUY at the strike price. You buy calls when you expect the price to rise.

2. If you buy a put option, you profit when the stock price:

A put gains value as the stock falls, so put buyers profit from downward moves.

3. You expect a company to do great and its stock to climb. Which fits your view?

A bullish outlook fits buying a call, which profits if the price rises.

4. What is the most a buyer can lose on an option they bought?

When you buy an option, the premium is your maximum loss if it expires worthless.

5. Why should beginners be cautious with options?

Options have a deadline and can go to zero. Learn stocks first and practice on paper before using real money.
