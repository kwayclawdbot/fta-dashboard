Green Team or Red Team?

Last lesson you labelled the parts — open, close, high, low, body, wicks. But a labelled candle is a scoreboard with no score. Today: call who WON, and by how much, in one glance.

Recalls · 2.4 The Six Parts ~3.5 min 2 games + quiz
Press play — learn to call the fight.
Signal 1 · Color

Green or red: who took the round

Opens at 50, closes at 54 — ended higher, prints green, buyers won. Opens at 50, closes at 47 — ended lower, prints red, sellers won. That's the whole rule.

Signal 2 · Body

The body says how hard they won

Long body = one side steamrolled, start to finish. Short body = a close fight, almost a draw. Body shrinks to nothing? That's a doji — a stalemate, and often a warning the move is running out of muscle.

Long body

Blowout win

Open and close far apart — high conviction, one-sided pressure.

Short body

Close fight

Close barely moved from the open — nobody really dominated.

Doji

Stalemate

Open ≈ close. The rope froze mid-fight — momentum may be fading.

Signal 3 · Wicks

Wicks are punches that got rejected

A long lower wick: sellers shoved price way down… and buyers slapped it right back before the close. The market tried a price and said no thanks. The wick shows the punch; the close shows who was standing at the bell.

Watch It Being Born

A candle, forming tick by tick

Reading finished candles is good. Watching one form is better. Run the day: the open locks, price wanders, wicks stretch, the body fills — and at the last tick the color locks. Then call the winner.

Lock It In

Quick check

Four questions on calling the fight.

1. A candle opens at $30 and closes at $33. It prints…

Close above open = green = buyers took the round. That's the entire color rule.

2. A LONG body tells you the round was…

Open and close far apart = strong one-sided pressure. Short body = close fight.

3. A candle with a tiny body and a long LOWER wick usually means…

The lower wick is the failed punch down; the close snapping back up shows buyers standing at the bell.

4. A doji — open and close at nearly the same price — signals…

A doji is the rope frozen mid tug-of-war. It warns momentum may be fading — a hint, never a promise.
