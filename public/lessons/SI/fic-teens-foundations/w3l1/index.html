Charts aren't chaos

A chart can look like pure noise. But the same shapes show up again and again, because crowds repeat themselves. Learn to name those shapes, then layer a few simple math helpers on top to read what the crowd is really doing.

Shapes the Crowd Draws

Patterns are repeatable shapes

A pattern is a shape a crowd keeps drawing on a chart. Some flip the trend — a reversal. Some just pause it, then keep going — a continuation.

Two reversal patterns · illustrative
A "W" says up. An "M" says down.
Double bottom (W) · reversal up  ·  Double top (M) · reversal down
Reversal

The trend flips

A double bottom — that W — hints a fall is turning into a rise. A double top — the M — warns a rise may be turning into a drop.

Continuation

The trend rests, then runs

A flag is a tight little pause right after a big move, like the stock catching its breath before it keeps going the same way.

Three Simple Helpers

Indicators layer math on price

Patterns read the shape. Indicators are simple math helpers drawn right on the price to confirm what you're already seeing.

A moving average is the average price drawn as one smooth line.

It cuts through the day-to-day noise so the trend direction is easy to see. A rising average points up; a falling one points down.
RSI · the rubber band · illustrative
RSI 50 · Neutral
Drag the slider. Below 30 the band is stretched down and may bounce; above 70 it's stretched up and may cool off. A clue, not a promise.
RSI · momentum

The rubber band

Stretch price too far, too fast, and momentum gets tight — possibly overbought or oversold — and tends to snap back. RSI measures how stretched it is.

Volume · conviction

Size of the crowd

Volume is how many shares actually traded. A move on huge volume has real conviction. The same move on a thin, quiet day is easy to doubt.

These shapes, curves, and gauges are illustrative teaching pictures, not a forecast or a promise. Patterns and indicators are clues you use to confirm an idea, never crystal balls.

Lock It In

Quick check

Five questions. Read each one like a real chart in front of you.

1. A stock falls, dips to the same floor twice forming a "W", then pushes up above the middle peak. This double bottom is:

A double bottom (the W) is a reversal up: price held the same floor twice and buyers took back control.

2. Price rallies to the same ceiling twice, gets rejected both times forming an "M", then rolls over. A double top warns of:

A double top (the M) is a reversal down: two rejections at the same ceiling warn the rise may be flipping to a fall.

3. What does a moving average do to a jagged price line?

A moving average draws the average price as one smooth line, cutting the noise so the trend direction stands out.

4. RSI pushes up near the top of its range and the rally stalls. That stretched reading is a clue the stock might be:

A high RSI is like a stretched rubber band: the move may be overheated and tend to snap back. It's a clue about momentum, not a promise.

5. A stock breaks above resistance. What makes that breakout more believable?

Volume is the proof. A breakout backed by a big crowd is far more trustworthy than one on thin, quiet volume.
